# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Plain static HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step, opened from a folder; `serve.py` (Python standard library) saves progress to `progress/progress.json`, runs exercise unit tests and runs playground code locally. Inferred and recommended: the stack question was put to the user on 2026-11-09 and the build proceeds on this answer until they reply.

## Users

People learning machine learning systems engineering on their own, from the requester and their team (lukasz, "we") who want to study the whole MLSysBook curriculum without losing track of where they are. They work in sessions of 15 to 45 minutes on a laptop, sometimes across many weeks, and often come back after a break needing to know exactly what they finished and what is next. (Inferred from the request: "similar to Codecademy with detailed examples, explanations and practice and test for everything", "detailed progress and memory so we don't lose track".)

## Product Purpose

One coherent course that turns the four MLSysBook volumes (Introduction, Scaling, Agentic, Physical AI), the TinyTorch build-it-yourself framework and the labs into lessons, interactive examples, practice, playgrounds and tests. Success: a learner can open the course, see their exact position, learn a concept, try it hands-on in a playground, prove it with a quiz or unit test, and see that recorded.

## Positioning

It is the only course that pairs the MLSysBook's quantitative "constraints drive architecture" teaching with running the numbers yourself (calculators and Python playgrounds) and building the machinery yourself (TinyTorch exercises checked by the module's own unit tests), all in one learning arc.

## Operating Context

- Generated by the book-to-course skill: content lives in `content/<chapter>/*.json`, built to `course/data.js` by `build_course.py`; exercises live in `exercises/<dir>/` with `_solution/`.
- Two content layers per chapter: "imported" (quiz banks and concept maps from the textbook repo) and "deep" (hand-written explanations, walkthroughs, widgets). The interface must show which layer a chapter has.
- Started with `./start.sh` or `start.bat` (Python 3); can also open `index.html` directly with progress in the browser only.
- Network may be absent: nothing essential may depend on a CDN.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Lessons: text, callouts, code, steppers, derivations, flows, reveals, tables, SVG diagrams, quizzes (single, multi, true/false, fill, order, numeric), flashcards, exercises, summaries, chapter tests.
- Playgrounds: in-page Python editor that runs through the local helper; interactive calculators for systems math (iron law, roofline, memory footprint, quantization and similar).
- Progress: per lesson, quiz, test, exercise and flashcard set, saved to a file through the helper and mirrored in the browser.
- Scale: 91 chapters, about 1,100 lessons. Navigation must stay usable at that size.
- Licences: textbook text is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (explain in own words, short attributed excerpts, quiz banks reused verbatim with attribution); TinyTorch is MIT; labs are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

## Product Principles

1. Never lose the learner's place: progress is visible everywhere and survives restarts.
2. Every concept gets a way to do something with it: a question, a calculation, a playground or a test.
3. Numbers you can recompute beat claims you must trust.
4. Honest about depth: an imported chapter says it is imported; a deep chapter earns the label.
5. Works offline from a folder.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard operable throughout, reduced-motion respected, readable long-form text on dark and light themes. No stricter standard was set.
